# High-signal copy frameworks (from market practice)

These are the frameworks people actually teach and ship for landing pages — use **with** StoryBrand and Don’t Make Me Think, not instead of them.

Sources: widely shared practitioner patterns (WHO/WHY/WHAT, benefit-first, objection→solution, outcome sell, words-before-design, PAS).

---

## Rule zero: words before design

Great design cannot save bad copy.  
Isa drafts the **scan layer** (H1, sub, proof label, plan, CTA) **before** Frontend or Craft invents layout.

---

## 1. WHO / WHY / WHAT (headline formula)

| Slot | Question | Example (Panel) |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| **WHO** | Specific audience | “founders shipping with AI agents” |
| **WHY** | Outcome they want | “catch friction before buyers bounce” |
| **WHAT** | Product in plain words | “a buyer panel that lives in your repo” |

**Template:**  
`[WHAT] for [WHO] who need [WHY]`  
or  
`[WHO]: [WHY] — [WHAT]`

Fail if WHO is “everyone” or WHAT is a metaphor only.

---

## 2. Benefit ladder (features are not enough)

People buy what they believe the product will **do for them**, not the ingredient list.

| Layer | Question | Panel example |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| Feature | What is it? | Multi-persona seats + report |
| Advantage | Why better? | Priority council, not one average reviewer |
| Benefit | Life better how? | Know the P0s tonight; ship with file paths |
| Identity | Who do they become? | Founder who doesn’t guess at conversion |

Every feature bullet must climb at least to **Benefit**.  
If it only says “AI-powered multi-agent system,” rewrite.

---

## 3. Objection → solution

Name the fear, then answer with product proof (not slogans).

| Objection | Solution (proof) |
|-----------|------------------|
| “Another AI toy?” | Sample report with scores + file paths |
| “Will it rewrite my brand?” | Preserve list + install CTA stays |
| “Too heavy to start?” | One `npx` + Run a panel |
| “Looks like every SaaS page?” | Artifact-first demo, not icon cards |

Put the strongest objection in the **sub** or directly under the hero — not buried in FAQ only.

---

## 4. PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solution)

Fast alternate to full SB7 when the page is short:

1. **Problem** — external fact (buyers leave; UI “works”)  
2. **Agitate** — cost of waiting (another week of guesswork)  
3. **Solution** — product + plan + CTA  

Do not agitate into fear-mongering. One honest stake line is enough.

---

## 5. Sell the dream outcome (with proof)

Outcome line must be **specific and checkable**:

- Bad: “Unlock your product’s potential”  
- Good: “Report in minutes; two P0s you can paste into issues”

Pair every dream line with an **artifact** (sample, path, command).

---

## 6. Direct CTA labels

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| Get started | Copy install |
| Learn more | Sample report |
| Submit | Run a panel |
| Sign up free | `npx @tysongreenan/panel init` |

Verb + object. Prefer the real product action.

---

## 7. Storytelling (copy, not theater)

Storytelling here means **narrative structure** (hero, problem, guide, plan), not purple prose.

- Specific beats abstract  
- “You” beats “people”  
- One scene (first install, first bounce) beats a brand manifesto
